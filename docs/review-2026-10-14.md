# Practice reliability review

This review starts from the published v0.8.0 release. Changes are proposed on a separate branch, preserving the released app and localhost companion while the pull request is reviewed.

Four parallel reviews covered real-video tracking, established vision frameworks, Android/core reliability, and browser usability. Two private Pixel recordings and a locally transcribed 2-minute-19-second narrated sample were used. The narration supplies expected behavior for steady and faster circles, temporary occlusion, grid stops, antispin, and intentionally inaccurate circles. Expectations guide the investigation; they are not automatic ground-truth endpoint labels. Recordings, transcripts, extracted images, and intermediate analysis stay local.

## Implemented reliability fixes

| Problem | Change | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At 60 fps, fitting discarded the first quarter of a two-second movement and could shift the guide center. A camera stall could also complete an incomplete fit. | Retain the entire capture interval and restart collection after a gap or clock reversal. | Known antispin trajectories at 30, 60, and 120 fps; interrupted and duplicate-timestamp samples. |
| Display-only settings silently reset manual beat synchronization. | Preserve the beat origin when changing presentation; clear crossing history when geometry changes. | Native/browser timing regressions with a known beat origin and target crossings. |
| A queued frame from an old camera source could mutate newly reset tracking state. | Recheck source generation inside the processing lock; stamp demo frames before processing. | Deterministic emulator test queues an old frame across a lifecycle reset. |
| Expired or repeated observations could retain confirmation history and validate a new object immediately. | Reset stale confirmation and motion history before reacquiring. | Matching native/browser tests for long gaps, repeated timestamps, and backward clocks. |
| Selecting colored LEDs could hollow out a large, overexposed white end cap. | Preserve bounded bright interiors when selected color supports their boundary. | Native/browser cap-interior and unrelated-white-light regressions; replay with and without color filtering. |
| Speed history and coverage could count a blurred or occluded observation when its confidence field was high. | Apply visibility flags consistently to current precision, coverage, and speed history. | High-confidence uncertain observations remain excluded during loss and recovery. |
| A normal brief pass behind the body broke an earned streak after 350 ms. | Hold the counter and earned glow for up to 1.5 seconds, explicitly labeled STREAK HELD. | Continuous uncertain frames add no time or precision; visible off-guide tips end the streak immediately. |
| A disconnected PC camera left previous live scores and source labels visible. | Explicit camera lifecycle state clears stale measurements and restores the start controls. | Browser disconnect, hidden/pagehide/resume, and unsupported-camera flows. |
| Analysis failure could disable Stop while recording continued. Storage failure could hide the promised temporary recording export. | Finalize recording on fatal analysis failure; make controls follow recorder state; expose temporary video/analysis downloads even if storage cannot be read. | Worker-failure and storage-failure browser regressions. |
| Rejected settings could leave a checkbox or input displaying a value that was never applied. | Restore authoritative control values and disable incompatible camera changes during recording. | Focused control rollback and recording-state browser checks. |
| Camera-overlay labels were rasterized at the small analysis resolution. Long status text crowded mobile fullscreen. | Render overlays at display resolution and provide compact fullscreen feedback with optional details. | Desktop, high-density, narrow-screen, and short landscape visual checks. |

## Narrated sample findings

A circle was fitted to manually inspected visible cap positions in the 6–18 second interval, then kept fixed. Its normalized settings were center X 0.4914, center Y 0.5322, and size 0.4414. The brightness threshold was 250 with Any / rainbow colors. These settings were selected for this recording, not established as universal defaults.

| Interval | Frames with a measurement | Median measured circle deviation |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Slow circles, 6–18 seconds | 649 / 714 (90.9%) | 1.50% of diameter |
| Subsequent circles, 18–24 seconds | 347 / 357 (97.2%) | 2.52% |
| Faster circles, 24–37 seconds | 744 / 773 (96.2%) | 1.99% |
| Deliberately inaccurate motion, 120–132 seconds | 263 / 714 (36.8%) | 19.43% |

Sparse visible-cap checks outside the fit interval support the separation: 16 inspected endpoints from 22–36 seconds had median radial error 1.18% and maximum 5.89%; six endpoints from three later inaccurate frames had median 16.95% and minimum 7.53%. Labels are approximate, with roughly 2–3 analysis-pixel uncertainty, and were not blinded. A later change in body position also affects a fixed guide. These findings are neither a whole-video accuracy estimate nor validation that every intended move is classified correctly.

Threshold selection mattered more than replacing the connected-component implementation. At threshold 250 without a color filter, the review changes preserve the narrated clip's 5,204 measured frames out of 8,308. With pink/purple selected, the cap-interior fix changes region shapes: measured-frame availability goes from 4,446 to 4,977 at threshold 175, but from 5,182 to 4,717 at threshold 250. Counts alone cannot establish improvement. In the sparse threshold-250 color-filtered subset, mean endpoint error changes only from about 8.02 to 8.00 analysis pixels, and the worst error remains about 44 pixels where the white shaft merges into a bright background. The preprocessing defect is fixed; that larger visibility error remains.

## Framework decision

See [the vision framework comparison](vision-framework-review.md) for experiments and primary references. Replacing connected components with OpenCV reproduced existing detections and did not demonstrate a speed improvement in the measured desktop cases. Sparse optical flow could drift onto the background despite passing a forward/backward consistency check. Neither is sufficient evidence of visible staff ends.

Keep the current local detector for this change. A future framework integration needs an endpoint-quality or device-performance benefit demonstrated on held-out recordings. Blur-aware circle-sweep adherence is a separate possible measurement: a visible sweep may preserve radial information while instantaneous tip angle is unknown. It needs its own evidence and uncertainty model before it can influence feedback; it cannot supply reliable beat timing by itself.

## Limits

Measured-frame availability is not endpoint accuracy. Visual review still found false precise endpoints at body-occlusion boundaries and where a white shaft merges into bright background light. The current heuristic cannot reliably distinguish every such boundary from a physical tip. A hidden end, clipped cap, or broad integrated light sweep can also remain explicitly uncertain, and no implementation can recover arbitrary invisible motion from one RGB camera. The brief streak hold preserves continuity without turning missing observations into successful measurements. Physical camera latency and a representative annotated endpoint benchmark remain outstanding.

The released v0.8.0 artifacts, signing identity, checksums, matching companion source, and GitHub checks were verified before this review branch began. Review changes are intended for pull-request review, not automatic merge or publication.
