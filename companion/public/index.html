    How beat practice works

    Follow the end labeled BEAT TIP. Only that end is timed. Pass it across a numbered target on each blue pulse; either direction works. The pulse is visual, with no metronome sound.

    With 4 points and 120 BPM, pass top → right → bottom → left on successive beats: one full circle every 2 seconds. Start with 2 points for a simpler exercise. All target rings pulse together; the numbers label positions, not a required starting point.

    Alternate the two tips at the four outer ends of the cross: top → right → bottom → left, or reverse. Reach one outer end on each blue pulse. All four rings pulse together; start anywhere. At 120 BPM, one full cycle takes 2 seconds.

    The horizontal tip moves left/right; the vertical tip moves up/down. Timing uses their combined cycle phase to estimate each turnaround. Position is how close the arriving tip comes to that outer target. The pulse is silent.

    On beat means within 75 milliseconds early or late. On target means within your Target tolerance. These are separate checks. Hidden or unclear tips do not produce a beat result.

    With manual BPM, Start beat now resets the pulse; Tap tempo sets its speed from your taps. System audio follows its detected beat. Adjust Audio timing offset if the pulse consistently leads or lags the music.

    Allowed distance from the guide, as a percentage of its width (the diameter in Circles). Higher is more forgiving; it does not change detection.

    Cyan = newest → blue / violet → pink = older. Trails fade over 4.2 seconds; subtle highlights mark clear detections.

    Keep every tip on the guide. Effects build at 2, 5, and 10 seconds. Brief blur or hidden tips hold your place for up to 1.5 seconds without adding time. Leaving the guide ends the streak.

    Camera & tracking03
    LED colors to detect

    All colors

    Match your staff's current LED setting. Choose one or more colors to ignore other lights; pick two for differently colored staves. White can still match sunlight.

    Background snapshot

    Off. Every bright light in view can be mistaken for a staff.

    Ignore lights that never move, like a window or lamp. After you press Capture, move your staves out of view for 5 seconds and keep the camera still. Recapture after moving the camera or changing the lighting.

    Compares actual frame rates at 720p, 480p, 1080p, and 240p. Prefers detail only when frame rates are nearly equal.

    Camera mode results

    Shorter exposure reduces light smears; the image may become darker. Availability depends on the webcam.

    Widest supported angle by default. Changing zoom or mirror clears angle calibration.

    The staff's apparent end-to-end length in the camera image, as a percent of the image's shorter edge. Shorter lights are ignored. Example: 22% of a 1920 × 1080 video is 238 pixels. This is not the guide size or the staff's real length.

    Use a fixed camera and keep both staves fully in frame. Lower the threshold for dimmer LEDs.
